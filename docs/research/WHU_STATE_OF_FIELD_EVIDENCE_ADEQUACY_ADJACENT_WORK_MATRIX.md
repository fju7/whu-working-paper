# Adjacent-work matrix — evidence adequacy, retrieval completeness, and assurance

Date: `2026-09-26`
Scope: public evidence available through this date. This is a literature map, not an exhaustive
bibliometric review. Product descriptions establish public claims and mechanisms, not independently
verified performance.

## Constructs kept separate

1. **Conditional reasoning correctness** — is the answer correct given the evidence supplied?
2. **Evidence fidelity / grounding** — do claims accurately reflect cited or retrieved sources?
3. **Acquisition adequacy / completeness** — did the process retrieve the evidence needed for the
   decision or claim?
4. **Residual-omission assurance** — what warrants believing no still-unseen evidence would change the
   material claim?
5. **Escalation economics** — what coverage, review burden, latency, and cost buy a target residual risk?

## Strongest evidence against WHU's framing

1. **Redundancy:** the unknown-denominator stopping problem is explicit in systematic-review screening
   and TAR, where it is called high-recall/total-recall retrieval, recall estimation, stopping criteria,
   and estimation of residual relevant documents.
2. **Partial solution:** probability sampling and statistical stopping provide real case-level confidence
   about recall in bounded collections. WHU must not say “nobody can assure completeness.”
3. **Deployed attention to the problem:** Google’s sufficient-context agent, Anthropic’s parallel research
   architecture, and professional medical/legal RAG systems deliberately broaden retrieval and expose
   sources. WHU must not say deployed systems ignore evidence adequacy.
4. **Possible category error:** in open-world research, literal completeness is often neither definable nor
   necessary. The defensible target is decision-relative residual omission risk under a declared scope,
   not proof that every relevant item was found.
5. **Possible economic answer:** systems may reasonably choose source restriction, expert verification,
   and abstention rather than solve general open-world closure. That is a policy trade-off, not a failure
   to recognize the problem.

## Terminology decision

- Prefer established component terms: **high-recall retrieval**, **total recall**, **recall estimation**,
  **stopping criteria**, **residual relevant documents**, **context sufficiency**, **grounding/faithfulness**,
  **selective prediction**, **risk–coverage**, **scalable oversight**, and **assurance case**.
- Retain **evidence adequacy** only as a clearly defined WHU umbrella for decision-relative sufficiency,
  not as a novelty claim.
- Use **acquisition assurance** only as a local compositional label for evidence supporting a residual-
  omission claim; say that the components have established precedents.
- Avoid **capability–assurance gap** as if it were an established named phenomenon. Prefer **an observed
  mismatch between task capability and available validation/oversight**.
- Avoid **assurance horizon**. METR’s **task-completion time horizon** is established and the proposed
  phrase risks implying a validated analogous metric that WHU does not have.
