# Warranted Trust V1 — Decision

## Overall disposition

`V1_FRAMEWORK_NOT_USEFUL`

This is a negative conceptual result, not a claim that the motivating problem is unimportant. Simpler established concepts adequately capture the surviving structure, and V1 adds no demonstrated predictive or operational content that justifies a distinct framework.

## Decision basis

1. External reference labels can validate bounded assurance properties without direct self-certification, but they do not generally establish proposition-level epistemic warrant.
2. `SUPPORTED_FOR_RELIANCE` has no threshold independent of decision stakes; adding one undermines the claimed separation.
3. Epistemic support, decision policy, and authority are useful distinctions, but they are ordinary epistemology/measurement, decision theory, and governance—not three warrant types.
4. Long-horizon history can matter, but established provenance, process assurance, monitoring, configuration management, and compositional verification suffice conceptually.
5. The scalar trust horizon is unsupported; a multivariate risk/coverage surface is ordinary evaluation.
6. Capability–trust difference is plausibly the established validation/oversight bottleneck, not a new gap metric.
7. Verification debt was correctly dropped.
8. Independent assurance can be cheaper than reproduction for certificate-rich tasks, but that does not solve the open-world motivating class.

## Non-expert user test

The V1 framework as a distinct vocabulary does not materially help the sophisticated non-expert beyond a well-designed assurance report that already states validated scope, uncertainty, coverage, invalidators, decision assumptions, and authority. Such reports may be valuable. Calling them “warranted trust” risks converting narrow validated properties into broad endorsement.

The practical limit is explicit: for some open-world or expert-judgment tasks, independent validation may require near-reproduction. The user cannot be given a cheap general certificate merely by adding more process metadata.

## Strongest critique and counterexample

Strongest critique: V1's categorical epistemic reliance state requires the use-/loss-sensitive threshold it assigns to the downstream action layer.

Strongest counterexample: an assurance instrument is independently and perfectly estimated to have 50% consequential-defect risk among grants. It satisfies the literal “bounded risk” and disclosure requirements, yet does not support reliance on a granted proposition. Tightening the threshold imports the decision problem.

## Empirical-test decision

No `WARRANTED_TRUST_V1_MINIMAL_EMPIRICAL_TEST.md` was created. Section 56 permits it only under `V1_CONCEPTUALLY_COHERENT_AND_READY_FOR_MINIMAL_OPERATIONALIZATION`, which was not reached.

The narrower empirical question—whether specified assurance and trajectory features improve held-out risk/coverage or realized loss over established baselines at lower cost than reproduction—remains legitimate, but designing it would require separate authorization framed as evaluation of established methods rather than operationalization of V1.
