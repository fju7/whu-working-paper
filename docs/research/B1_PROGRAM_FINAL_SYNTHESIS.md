# B1 program final synthesis

## Answer

`BOUNDED_REFERENCE_RICH_SYNTHESIS_FEASIBILITY_DEMONSTRATED`

The bounded synthesis core has earned progression to one genuinely different feasibility
challenge. Under the favorable conditions actually tested—an exact, reference-rich, source-native,
professionally bounded aspirin corpus; frozen typed frameworks; and a heavy control stack—the cheap
model demonstrated evidence synthesis, valid citation enumeration, benefit/harm recognition,
abstention when a next-layer framework was absent, and correct application when it was supplied.

Evidence standard: a property is “demonstrated” only when a gate-eligible live output directly
exercised it against a prospectively frozen or independently authoritative reference, with exact
input/output durability. Mechanical custody and hash properties may be “mechanically verified.”
Diagnostic runs and post hoc forensics cannot create a live pass, but they may identify whether a
formal failure is evidence about the intended construct. Generalization beyond the tested scope is
not inferred.

This conclusion does not rewrite any historical failure and is narrower than the governing PRD's
full Phase B outcome, which also requires reader, comparator, independent-reference, burden, and
economic evidence.

## What B1 established

The program's chronology matters. Original B1 and v2.3 did not isolate model reasoning: lossy
semantic inputs, absent durable payloads, and a non-reconstructable response path came first.
Gate A/B then supplied source-native semantics and durable evaluation. V2.4 produced the protected
direction but exposed opaque citation-key copying and an all-number rule. V2.5 fixed the citation
contract, passed every citation, and revealed that the protected target improperly combined
evidence synthesis, Task Force net-benefit judgment, and clinical action. V2.6 separated those
layers and the model behaved correctly at each substantive joint.

The remaining v2.6 failures are important but different: scope/identity numerals were treated as
quantitative claims; two condition identifiers were swapped; and two correct boundary statements
carried a next-layer metadata label. They show that machine-readable contracts need semantic
guarding. They do not show a failed evidence synthesis, leaked answer, wrong net-benefit judgment,
or wrong recommendation transformation.

Current governing state: `HUMAN_REFERENCE_NOT_YET_REQUIRED` for progression to the next bounded

## Would another aspirin run change the decision?

`ANOTHER_ASPIRIN_SYNTHESIS_RUN_WOULD_NOT_MATERIALLY_CHANGE_THE_PRODUCT_DECISION`

The only crisp new hypothesis would be whether a revised numeric/typing/schema contract can make
the same aspirin benchmark formally green while eliciting a provenance-backed numerical effect.
That would be useful contract regression evidence, but it is not decision-critical for progression
of the bounded core. The substantive layer behaviors are already observed. A new aspirin run would
mostly optimize benchmark conformance and carries a clear pass-chasing/benchmark-overfitting risk.

## Next genuinely different challenge

**Acquisition adequacy**: can WHU identify and bind the right evidence for a declared question
instead of receiving a frozen professionally assembled corpus?

Do not design or execute that challenge here. It reduces more product uncertainty because B1's
positive evidence is conditional on an unusually favorable supplied corpus. Failure to find a
consequential study, correction, or contrary source would invalidate the product promise before
fine-grained synthesis-contract polish matters. The challenge should also expose whether the
control apparatus transfers or becomes uneconomic, but those are observations around the one
challenge class, not separate experiments.

## Falsifiers of the optimistic interpretation

The present conclusion should be reversed or narrowed if a prospectively frozen, independently
anchored test shows any of the following: the model omits or distorts a supplied consequential
source despite conserved inputs; crosses an absent framework boundary in substance; applies a
supplied framework incorrectly; fabricates a load-bearing quantitative result; fails on a material
domain/case change; or requires routine expert reconstruction/control labor that defeats the
economics. Acquisition failure on a consequential source would falsify progression toward an
end-to-end product even while leaving the narrow B1 result intact.

## Strategic product lessons

1. Cheap-model capability: in a bounded, reference-rich setting, a low-cost model can perform the
   substantive synthesis and typed transformations tested here. It is less reliable at opaque
   identifiers and machine metadata than at the prose reasoning.
2. Engineering controls: semantic conservation, exact identity, frozen execution, and durability
   were necessary to know what failed. Their value is demonstrated; their recurring cost is not.
3. Evaluation/reference design: reference type and evaluator construct were repeatedly decisive.
   A wrong target or overbroad regex can manufacture an apparent capability failure.
4. Epistemic-reliance thesis: reliable use depends on separating evidence findings, synthesis,
   net-benefit judgment, and action, and supplying explicit transformations between them. Local
   grounding alone does not license global warrant.
5. Falsification: consequential supplied-evidence errors, substantive boundary crossing, failed
   framework application, fabricated quantities, non-transfer, or uneconomic routine verification
   would defeat the optimistic interpretation.
6. Domain experts: B1 contains no evidence that every issue requires routine domain-expert
   verification. It also contains no evidence that experts are dispensable for all later targets.
7. Economics: yes—there is affirmative warning evidence that the control stack may threaten
   viability. Multiple failures were in machinery and measurement, and model-call cost was trivial
   relative to the unmeasured construction and maintenance burden.
